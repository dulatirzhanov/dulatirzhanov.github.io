About

I started as a teacher in a rural school, then worked in an urban school, in the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools system and its central office. I now lead school development at ISS Astana.

At NIS I worked on CIS accreditation for a network of 22 schools and on a national programme supporting 191 rural schools. As a Council of International Schools volunteer, I lead teams that evaluate international schools in different countries.

Quality in a school comes from decisions, and the best decisions I have seen were made by leaders who knew how other systems had solved the same problem. I write about that international practice and build tools that make it usable for school leaders.

I work in Kazakh, Russian and English, so practice from abroad reaches readers already adapted to their context.

What you can write to me about

You can discuss with me standards, school quality, institutional design (how decisions, responsibility and rules are set up in a school) and research on these topics. Anyone can write: a school head or owner, a deputy head, an accreditation coordinator, a teacher, a researcher or a student.

  • Standards and external review. What standards require, CIS included, how that shows in a school's daily work, and how to prepare for a review and for international accreditation.
  • Decisions, responsibility and rules. Who decides what, how the founder, board and head of school share responsibility, and where decisions are recorded.
  • Seeing the school clearly. An outside read of how the school is run, what its documents say and what is missing.
  • Strategy and development plans. How to turn intentions into priorities with owners and measures.
  • Policies and procedures. A coherent set of documents that describes how the school actually works.
  • Research and study. Questions for research or coursework on school quality and school management.
  • Regular conversations with the head of school. A standing thinking partner on current decisions who knows international practice.

I do not evaluate for CIS accreditation any school I have advised. Work with a school's documents begins with a confidentiality agreement.

First conversation

The first conversation is free and carries no obligation. A simple question is reason enough to write, and a few lines by email or WhatsApp will do. If further work is needed, we agree on terms after that conversation.

To prepare, you can fill in a short questionnaire about the school. It takes about ten minutes.
